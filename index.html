My approach

Design decisions, backed by how people behave

Most freelance sites are judged on how they look on day one. I design mine to keep working after that, built around what a visitor is actually trying to do when they land on the page, whether that's booking an appointment, finding a phone number, or trusting a business enough to reach out.

That comes from a background in data and behavioural analysis, applied to something more practical: a site that's easy to find your way around, loads fast, and gives customers what they came for.

  • Built around the visitor, not the sitemap Every page has one job, I design around that, not around a template's default sections.
  • Measurable from day one Basic tracking set up at launch, so you can see what's actually working, not guess.
  • Clear, honest communication Plain updates through the build, no jargon, no vanishing between milestones.
Why have a website?

Instagram can bring people to your business. A website gives them somewhere to go.

Social media is valuable for reaching people, building awareness and keeping customers engaged. But your Instagram page is still a space that exists on someone else's platform.

If your business relies entirely on social media, important information such as your services, prices, contact details and story can become difficult for customers to find. Platforms change, algorithms change, and accounts can sometimes become inaccessible.

A website gives your business its own central home online, somewhere you control, where customers can always learn about you, explore what you offer and take the next step.

  • You own the space Your website gives your business a dedicated online presence that isn't dependent on a social media feed or algorithm.
  • Everything has a place Services, pricing, booking information, FAQs, contact details and your business story can all live in one easy-to-find place.
  • It helps build trust A professional website gives potential customers a place to properly understand who you are before deciding whether to contact or buy from you.
  • Social media and websites work together Instagram can capture attention. Your website can turn that attention into an enquiry, booking or sale.

What does a visitor need first?

Most visitors want availability and price before anything else, so those sit above the founder's story, not below it. Small ordering decisions like this come from thinking about the visit, not the sitemap.

What's the one action?

A menu, a phone number, a way to book, every page gets one clear next step. If a site offers five competing links, most visitors take none of them.
